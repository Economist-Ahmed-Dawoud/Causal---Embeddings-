# Literature Review: Causal Inference with Text Embeddings and Double Machine Learning

## The Omitted Variable Bias Problem

Omitted variable bias (OVB) remains one of the most persistent challenges in observational causal inference. When a confounder affects both treatment assignment and outcomes but remains unobserved in structured data, naive regression estimates become severely biased. In digital labor markets, unobservable worker characteristics—such as "savviness," "professionalism," or "intrinsic ability"—create precisely this problem, confounding the relationship between program participation and earnings outcomes.

## Double Machine Learning: High-Dimensional Causal Inference

**Chernozhukov et al. (2018)** revolutionized causal inference in high-dimensional settings with Double Machine Learning (DML). Their seminal work in *The Econometrics Journal* introduced a framework for estimating low-dimensional causal parameters θ₀ in the presence of high-dimensional nuisance parameters η₀. The key innovation is **Neyman orthogonality**: by using cross-fitting and machine learning methods (Lasso, Random Forests, Neural Networks) to estimate nuisance functions, DML achieves √n-consistent and asymptotically normal estimates even when the dimension of controls vastly exceeds the sample size.

Recent extensions include **Bach et al. (2024)** on hyperparameter tuning for DML, and **Chernozhukov et al. (2024)** published a comprehensive book *Applied Causal Inference Powered by ML and AI* (CausalML-book.org), demonstrating DML's applicability across domains. The DoubleML software package (Bach et al., 2024, *Journal of Statistical Software*) has become the standard implementation, supporting LassoCV for L1-regularized high-dimensional control.

## Text as Data: A New Frontier

The "Text as Data" movement, pioneered by **Grimmer, Roberts, and Stewart (2022, 2024)**, recognizes that unstructured text contains rich information about latent variables. Their framework emphasizes that while text is inherently high-dimensional, it can be systematically analyzed using machine learning to extract meaningful measures.

**Egami et al. (2022)** in *Science Advances* ("How to make causal inferences using texts") introduced a split-sample workflow for causal inference with text, warning that naive use of discovered text measures risks overfitting and identification failures. Their work emphasizes the need for **pre-analysis commitment** and **causal assumptions** when treating text-derived measures as treatments or outcomes.

## Causal Embeddings: Bridging Text and Confounding

The breakthrough came with **Veitch, Sridhar, and Blei (2020)** in their UAI paper "Adapting Text Embeddings for Causal Inference." They formalized the concept of **causally sufficient embeddings**: low-dimensional representations of text that preserve only the information needed for causal adjustment—specifically, features predictive of both treatment and outcome.

Their key insight: while text T may be prohibitively high-dimensional (e.g., raw vocabularies), **supervised dimensionality reduction** can extract embeddings W that suffice for controlling confounding. By adapting deep language models (e.g., transformers) with a multi-task loss predicting both D and Y, they construct embeddings that satisfy the backdoor criterion: (Y ⊥ D | W, X).

**Recent advances (2024-2025)** include:
- **DoubleLingo (2024, NAACL)**: Combines LLM-based nuisance models with DML, achieving 10.4% error reduction in causal estimates
- **LLMs for Missing Variable Identification (2025, arXiv)**: Uses LLMs as proxies to hypothesize hidden confounders in causal graphs
- **Using LLMs for Conditional Expectation (2025)**: Shows LLM-powered predictions can improve causal estimates in double machine learning when high-dimensional confounders are present

## The Gap Our Paper Fills

Despite these advances, **no existing work has demonstrated the full pipeline** of using LLM-generated text embeddings as proxies for latent confounders within a rigorous DML framework, benchmarked against a known ground truth. Existing applications either:
1. Use text as the *treatment* or *outcome*, not as a proxy for confounders (Egami et al.)
2. Demonstrate the method on observational data without known causal effects (Veitch et al.)
3. Focus on theoretical properties without empirical validation on realistic data (DoubleLingo)

Our contribution is **methodological validation**: we construct a synthetic Data Generating Process (DGP) where:
- The true causal effect τ = $5.00 is known by design
- A latent confounder U (freelancer ability) is unobserved in tabular data
- U is *observable* via text T (profile descriptions) generated by LLMs to reflect ability
- We benchmark three approaches: Naive OLS (biased), DML with text embeddings (theoretically unbiased), and Bayesian inference with PCA-reduced embeddings (quantifies uncertainty)

This provides **proof of concept** that modern NLP embeddings, when properly integrated into econometric frameworks, can recover causal effects corrupted by unobserved confounding—a critical challenge in gig economy research, hiring discrimination studies, and any domain where human characteristics are better captured in text than in structured variables.
